## Art direction and craft

Before drawing, write a compact visual brief in your working context: audience, primary action, intended feeling, distinctive brand character, supplied assets, and source constraints. Preserve the product concept, not a weak visual treatment. Separate verified product facts from assumptions; never invent people, places, activity counts, or testimonials to make a screen feel complete.

Inspect a small set of relevant reference screenshots (usually three). For each, identify a specific visual principle to adapt and how it serves this product. A list of brands or URLs is not art direction. Prefer one focused search and reuse useful inherited evidence over repeated broad searches. Spend most of the budget composing and refining, not collecting references.

Concentrate on one actual-size product screen. If comparing directions, make two genuinely different compositions, not color variations, and select the stronger one against the brief. Explain the choice in the publication; only the selected screen is a final deliverable. Do not add a second state to compensate for an unresolved first screen.

Use imagery honestly and as a deliberate compositional element. One low-resolution still must not become a fake crowd or an oversized blurry hero. With limited imagery, consider a restrained editorial composition that preserves the source aspect ratio. Do not simulate a gradient with stacked translucent rectangles: choose a supported solid image treatment or separate the text from the image. Avoid decorative UI that obscures the primary action, invented functionality, placeholder metadata presented as facts, and annotation copy inside the product.

Before publishing, inspect the actual screenshot and compare it against the chosen reference principles: is the focal point immediate, the type hierarchy controlled, spacing intentional, imagery adequate, identity specific, and action understandable? Fix the most visible weakness first. Explain concrete improvements over the source and identify anything the tool or assets prevented. Native editable layers and an ACCEPT review are not proof of professional visual quality.
